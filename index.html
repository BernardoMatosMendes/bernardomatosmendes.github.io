Publications

Inventory Decisions Under Political Violence

with Cláudia Custódio and Diogo Mendes

Economics Letters, 247 (2025), 112158

Coverage: Financial Times, The FinReg Blog (Duke)

We estimate the effect of violent political conflicts on firm inventory purchase decisions using monthly data of 431 clients of a multinational beverage supplier in Mozambique. Firms reduce inventory purchases by up to 19% in response to conflicts occurring within a 10 km radius. This is observed exclusively among small firms, which reduce their purchases by 28%–32% compared to larger firms. Small firms are also more likely to temporarily or permanently halt their purchases. However, conditional on survival, the effect is short-lived. Our results underscore the disproportionate impact of political violence on small firms, potentially widening the gap between small and large businesses in developing economies.

Green Mortgages

with João F. Cocco and S. Lakshmi Naaraayanan

CEPR Discussion Paper DP21346, March 2026

Coverage: E-Axes Forum

We study contractual features and lenders’ motivations for offering Green mortgages, used to finance energy-efficient properties, in the United Kingdom. There is significant heterogeneity in benefits: preferential-rate mortgages offer 9bp discounts, while the benefits are substantially lower for products with upfront cashback. Our evidence shows that the green label functions as a tool for product differentiation and customer acquisition, with lenders tailoring product features around borrower heterogeneity. In particular, we show that they use upfront cashback as a salient pricing feature of credit contracts to target liquidity-constrained homebuyers. We find no evidence that the financial benefits reflect lower credit risk.

Bank Advertising and Deposit Demand

Single-authored

Coverage: American Banker

I provide empirical evidence that bank advertising impacts deposit volumes. Exploiting spatial discontinuities in advertising created by the geographical segmentation of the US local TV market, I show that increased bank advertising raises branch-level deposits. The estimated elasticity to advertising spending is 0.04, suggesting sizable returns. The impact is stronger in wealthier and more educated counties, and among smaller banks, consistent with advertising raising awareness and reducing search frictions. At the aggregate level, bank advertising contributes to the overall expansion of deposits. These results highlight the understudied role of advertising in driving customer acquisition in the deposit market.

Credit Access and Market Access

with Cláudia Custódio and Christopher Hansman

CEPR Discussion Paper No. 19049, May 2024

Coverage: Portuguese Economy Research Report

We show that credit allows firms to enter export markets. We employ a regression discontinuity design based on the eligibility criteria for a government program that provided credit guarantees to Portuguese SMEs. Qualifying firms access credit and become exporters, expanding both the scale and scope of their international presence. The effects are persistent: firms that are temporarily eligible for the guarantee continue to export even after losing access. This evidence is consistent with trade models in which financing frictions distort participation at the extensive margin. Our findings underscore the role of credit-focused policies as a tool for promoting international trade.
